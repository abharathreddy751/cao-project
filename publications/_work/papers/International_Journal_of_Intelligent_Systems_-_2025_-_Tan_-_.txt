Heterogeneous multicore processor systems are commonly used for scheduling tasks of DAG applications. Deep reinforcement
learning, with its superior ability to perceive decisions directly and handle high-dimensional state actions, has become a prevalent
solution for scheduling these systems. However, the incomplete environment models and large action spaces of deep re-
inforcement learning present signifcant challenges to scheduling. Tis paper investigates a scheduling problem in a heteroge-
neous multicore processor environment. Initially, system environment information is extracted and encoded using a graph
convolutional neural network based on integrating adapter and AdapterFusion into the transformer architecture. Ten, by
separating task selection and processor allocation, the decision space is reduced: the former uses a deep neural network to learn to
select nodes, and the latter allocates processors using a heuristic scheduling algorithm combining earliest completion time-based
node replication and rolling technology. Te entire scheduling process is a Markov decision problem. Terefore, the PPO al-
gorithm with dynamic adjustment of the clipping factor, combined with an advantage actor-critic network, is employed for
training, optimizing, and evaluating the algorithm to fnd the optimal scheduling strategy. Te training process adopts a reward
function for the time and power consumption required for completed task scheduling to ensure that multiple DAG application
task scheduling can achieve optimal performance. Experiments conducted in various environments with diferent parameters
show that, compared to other algorithms, this algorithm reduces the overall execution time and power consumption cost of
heterogeneous multicore processor tasks by 11.09%.
Keywords: advantage actor-critic network; DAG scheduling; heterogeneous multicore processor systems; PPO algorithm;
transformer architecture
1. Introduction
With the continuous development and expansion of 5G, new
devices are constantly connecting to the Internet, enabling
the integration of people, machines, and objects, thereby
achieving the Internet of Everything. Consequently, data
transmission has become more frequent, and application
scenarios have become more complex and diverse, leading to
the delivery of a large number of heterogeneous tasks [1] in
5G communication networks. Heterogeneous tasks typically
feature diferent characteristics, requirements, and execu-
tion environments within a heterogeneous task set. More-
over, a large number of interdependent heterogeneous tasks
exist within the same task set. For example, video or audio
data tasks transmitted through broadband communication
require
real-time
computation
and
transmission,
de-
terministic communication control tasks, and communi-
cation protocol tasks that need adaptation and conversion
between diferent protocols. Tese heterogeneous tasks’
structures can be represented as directed acyclic graphs
(DAGs), allowing for more convenient allocation of tasks to
diferent types of processors for accelerated processing. Such
an environment forms a heterogeneous multicore processor
system [2, 3].
Heterogeneous
multicore
processor
systems
are
designed to accelerate the resolution of diferent types of

research on scheduling algorithms in heterogeneous mul-

Scheduling
algorithms
generally
fall
into
machine
learning–based and traditional scheduling algorithms [6].
Currently, the most commonly used traditional scheduling
algorithms are heuristic scheduling algorithms, which use
existing information for reasoning and computation to obtain
approximate optimal solutions [7]. For example, Wang et al.
[8] proposed describing task scheduling in a heterogeneous
multicore processor system environment as a binary non-
linear programming (BNP) problem [9]. Based on the Least
Slack Time First (LSTF) and Earliest Deadline First (EDF),
they designed a heuristic task scheduling method, which
performs well in minimizing task violations and resource
utilization performance. However, due to the typically dy-
namic nature of heterogeneous system environments, where
task arrival times and resource availability can change at any
time, this algorithm may not quickly adapt to dynamic
changes, leading to suboptimal scheduling and potential
recomputation situations. Li et al. [10] proposed a method to
reduce communication costs by bundling tasks with high
communication costs on a single node and employing
bundling scheduling strategies and replication mechanisms to
reduce communication delays. However, in environments
with increasing numbers of heterogeneous system tasks, over-
reliance on task bundling reduces scheduling fexibility, and
bundling tasks with high communication costs may lead to
certain nodes being overloaded while others are idle, afecting
overall system efciency and performance. Heuristic sched-
uling algorithms are prone to local optima, especially in large
heterogeneous multicore processor systems, where local
optima can lead to overall performance degradation.
With the upgrade of hardware architecture and device
capabilities, deep learning models in machine learning are
favored for their strong adaptability, ability to capture and
handle complex nonlinear relationships, automatic extrac-
tion of high-dimensional features, powerful prediction ca-
pabilities, and self-improving scheduling strategies [11].
Researchers have increasingly favored deep learning–based
scheduling algorithms. Currently, these algorithms have
been applied to many complex task scheduling scenarios
with good results. However, many deep learning–based
scheduling algorithms do not simultaneously consider time
and energy consumption for heterogeneous system envi-
ronments, and their ability to work in multitask and
multiworkfow environments is often insufcient [12]. In
real system environments, multiple task fows coexist, en-
larging the scheduling decision space and increasing com-
putational complexity. Tis requires neural networks with
multiple fully connected (FC) layers for function ftting and
more efcient algorithm strategies. Furthermore, many al-
gorithms using stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for
training are prone to local optima. Terefore, many re-
searchers propose using proximal policy optimization (PPO)
to efectively reduce the probability of local optima. Tis
paper focuses on this aspect of research.
Tis paper focuses on the structure of heterogeneous tasks
in DAGs using graph convolutional neural networks and
combining improved PPO reinforcement learning algo-
rithms. Te proposed algorithm extracts subsequent required
feature information from the DAG using graph convolutional
neural networks, and then continuously optimizes and trains
the deep neural network to select task nodes. Finally, the
heuristic algorithm combining node replication and rolling
technology based on the earliest completion time determines
the optimal processor allocation strategy. Heuristic algo-
rithms are employed to reduce the decision space and im-
prove algorithmic stability. Te performance is measured
based on three key indicators: the maximum makespan, the
average speedup ratio, and the average power consumption.
Experimental results show that this algorithm outperforms
existing scheduling algorithms. In summary, the main con-
tributions of this paper are as follows:
1. In the feature extraction stage of DAG task nodes,
a graph convolutional neural network combined with
a transformer architecture is applied to the DAG
structure of heterogeneous tasks.
2. In the training and selection of nodes, a FC neural
network is used for selection, employing an advantage
actor-critic (A2C) algorithm combined with a PPO
algorithm based on dynamic attenuation clipping
functions, improving training efciency, enhancing
system stability, and reducing the probability of the
optimal scheduling strategy falling into local optima.
3. Reconstructing the reward function, using time and
energy consumption for weight synthesis, and en-
abling the generation of the optimal scheduling
strategy are based on actual constraint conditions of
time and energy consumption. Experimental results
show that the algorithm improves performance by
11.09%.

Classical scheduling algorithms typically adopt a design
paradigm rooted in linear analysis of task parameters. As
a quintessential representative of traditional scheduling
methodologies, the Shortest Job First (SJF) algorithm [13]
operates through constructing priority queues based on job
execution duration, employing heuristic selection mecha-
nisms for node scheduling. While demonstrating notable
advantages in temporal complexity and implementation
simplicity, this algorithmic framework exclusively relies on
temporal dimension as the singular optimization metric,
failing to incorporate topological constraint relationships
between tasks into its computational model. Such inherent
characteristics lead to substantial performance degradation
in heterogeneous system architectures with multidimen-
sional constraints, particularly manifesting in distributed
computing
scenarios
containing
strongly-coupled
de-
pendency relationships.
Kaur et al. [14] proposed a reinforcement workfow
scheduling algorithm named deep Q-learning-based het-
erogeneous earliest fnish time (DQ-HEFT), which combines
deep Q-learning with the HEFTalgorithm. Te algorithm frst
uses the HEFT algorithm to update Q-table values as im-
mediate rewards. Ten, it sorts the tasks according to the
converged Q-table to obtain the optimal policy. Finally, the
algorithm employs the EFTallocation technique to assign the
best processor to each task. Since the task scheduling problem
in a heterogeneous multicore processor system environment
is a multiobjective problem, Sahoo et al. [15] proposed
a dynamic task scheduling algorithm based on existing meta-
heuristic
algorithms
[16]
and
deep
recurrent
neural
network–black widow optimizer (DRNN-BWO). Tis algo-
rithm prioritizes tasks directly using an upward ranking
technique and maps them to suitable multiobjective pro-
cessors in order to minimize the maximum completion time
and maximize processor utilization.
Moreover, the DAG of heterogeneous tasks contains all
the information needed for scheduling tasks. Song et al. [17]
proposed a scheduling algorithm for heterogeneous multi-
core processor systems based on a neural network learning
model. Tis algorithm extracts key feature information from
the DAG using graph convolution and learns the order of
node selection from this feature information. Subsequently,
it employs a heuristic algorithm to assign tasks to
determined nodes.
According to the research outcomes of the above-
mentioned studies, the scheduling process is generally di-
vided into two parts: task node selection and processor
allocation.
However, these studies do not consider dynamically
adjusting the strategy to reduce the probability of the
scheduling strategy falling into a local optimum. Terefore,
this paper will focus on research in this area.
Moreover, recent advances have witnessed the emer-
gence of graph transformer architectures for scheduling
heterogeneous DAG workloads, aiming to capture both local
graph structure and long-range dependencies with greater
fdelity. For instance, Li et al. [18] propose Graphormer-
Scheduler, which converts each DAG into a FC graph
augmented with positional encodings and applies multiple
layers of Graphormer (graph Transformer) to compute
global self-attention scores. While Graphormer-Scheduler
improves makespan by modeling long-range interactions, it
sufers from high computational complexity—its full self-
attention over all nodes scales as O(N2)—and it lacks
a mechanism to mitigate catastrophic forgetting when fne-
tuning on diverse DAG instances. Moreover, Graphormer-
Scheduler focuses exclusively on makespan optimization
without explicitly incorporating energy consumption into its
objective.
Chen et al. [19] introduce AttentionFlow, a hybrid graph
attention network (GAT) + Transformer model in which
a two-layer Transformer encoder is stacked atop a GAT to
generate
task
priority
embeddings
for
reinforcement
learning–based scheduling. Although AttentionFlow dem-
onstrates approximately 9% improvement in makespan
compared to standard GAT or HEFT baselines, its design
reveals two major shortcomings: (1) the GAT and Trans-
former layers are loosely coupled, causing suboptimal fusion
of local topological features and high-order global de-
pendencies; (2) the model’s performance and stability are
highly sensitive to Transformer depth and number of at-
tention heads, making it difcult to generalize across dif-
fering DAG sizes and communication-to-computation ratio
(CCR). Furthermore, AttentionFlow’s reward function
adopts only a rudimentary linear delay penalty, providing
limited support for multiobjective trade-ofs between latency
and energy.
Kumar and Singh [20] develop a GCN-Transformer
hybrid tailored for cloud task scheduling, where node
embeddings from a multilayer GCN are fed into a stan-
dard Transformer encoder to capture global task corre-
lations. Tis approach partially alleviates the locality
limitation of pure GCNs. However, full fne-tuning of all
Transformer parameters introduces two critical issues: (1)
training instability and tendency toward overftting when
deployed on large-scale heterogeneous DAGs, due to the
substantial number of trainable parameters; (2) a lack of
lightweight adaptation modules, resulting in poor online
transferability and large performance variance across data
distributions.
In contrast to the above methodologies, our work
proposes a GCN + AdapterFusion–Transformer framework
that addresses the highlighted limitations. Specifcally:

4. Proposed Algorithm
Tis paper proposes a scheduling algorithm for heteroge-
neous multicore processor systems based on DRL. Te al-
gorithm is suitable for distributed operating systems on
multiprocessor platforms. Te algorithm’s framework is
depicted in Figure 1 and is divided into three main com-
ponents: graph convolutional network (GCN), advantage
A2C network, and heuristic scheduling algorithm. Ac-
cordingly, the process can be divided into three stages.
Te feature representation phase initiates by processing
native node attributes from DAG workloads through a hy-
brid architecture integrating GCN with Transformer-based
contextual encoders. Te GCN efectively extracts key fea-
tures from this data as output. Next, the extracted key
features are input into the node network for learning and
optimization, which selects nodes as output for the next
stage. Finally, the heuristic scheduling algorithm assigns the
selected nodes to the most suitable processors. Tis stage
involves continuous parameter optimization using the A2C
algorithm combined with an improved PPO algorithm. Tis
process repeats until all nodes are assigned to appropriate
processors.
4.1. Topological Attribute Abstraction via Transformer-GCN
Synergy. Fundamental to our algorithmic operation is
system-wide task ontology synthesis for scheduling opti-
mization. Structural embodiment emerges in the inter-
connected component mapping presented as shown in
Figure 2. In a heterogeneous multicore processor system
with multiple tasks and workfows, the number of tasks is
dynamically changing, and the dependency relationships
between tasks are complex. Additionally, at specifc times,
certain tasks may arrive or complete.
Consequently, the variable dimensionality inherent to
raw feature inputs demands an adaptive processing mech-
anism capable of maintaining representational fdelity. Es-
sential DAG attributes–encompassing task execution costs,
internodal
communication
latencies,
and
topological
dependencies–require transformation via graph convolu-
tional operators for hierarchical feature abstraction and
high-dimensional embedding learning. Tis process yields
scalable task state information, facilitating the subsequent
handling of task nodes by the node selection neural network.
Traditional graph convolution methods predominantly
aggregate information recursively layer by layer to extract
feature information. Tis approach is often suboptimal in
terms of parallel efciency and capturing high-level features
[22]. Currently, due to the outstanding performance of
transformer models based on self-attention mechanisms in
these two aspects, researchers are extensively applying them
to GCNs [23].
Te transformer model used in this paper is illustrated in
the fgure below. By incorporating AdapterFusion from
Pfeifer et al. [24], the learning process is divided into two
stages to enhance downstream task performance. Tese two
stages are the knowledge extraction stage and the knowledge
combination stage.
In the knowledge extraction stage, training is aimed at
enabling downstream applications to acquire task-specifc
knowledge. Tis requires training adapter modules to en-
capsulate this knowledge within their parameters. In the
knowledge combination stage, to improve model perfor-
mance on the target task, it is necessary to fne-tune both the
pretrained model parameters and the task-specifc adapter
parameters. Additionally, new parameters are introduced to
learn how to efectively combine knowledge from multiple
adapters.
By applying AdapterFusion from this model, this paper
efectively addresses issues such as catastrophic forgetting,
task interference, and training instability, resulting in more
accurate training data.

4.3. Computational Resource Assignment Using Blended
Search Strategies. Following the derivation of node sched-

vertices necessitate binding to processing units to augment
systemic throughput in heterogeneous multicore architec-

with the cardinality of schedulable nodes and available
processors. Such combinatorial expansion imposes com-

mately degrading scheduling efcacy. Consequently, this

during vertex discernment, topological attributes exclusively
inform prioritization while processor metadata remain la-

Tere are generally three types of heuristic algorithms.
List-based heuristic algorithms usually implement the
earliest fnish time method, which has relatively poor
performance. For instance, Topcuoglu et al. [25] proposed
a typical list-based heuristic algorithm that implements
the earliest fnish time method. Task replication-based
heuristic algorithms replicate parent nodes onto the same
processor according to certain rules to reduce commu-
nication overhead and are often used in conjunction with
other heuristic algorithms [26]. Meta-heuristic algo-
rithms, through random search, combine or mutate
feasible solutions during the search process to achieve
optimal performance [27].

training, this research implements a synergistic scheduling
paradigm integrating Earliest Finish Time heuristics with
topological replication mechanisms. Te computational
sequence operates under the following protocol.

consisting of nodes that are currently executable or have
satisfed all dependency constraints. For each candidate task,

actual execution time.

processors, thereby reducing overall execution time. How-

distributing tasks across multiple processors. To ensure that
node replication efectively advances the earliest fnish time

tasks, the scheduling approach must incorporate a quanti-
tative balancing mechanism.

4.5. Computational Complexity. Te algorithmic research
comprises three phases. In the feature extraction phase, task
node features are extracted from DAGs by integrating GCNs
and Transformer modules. For a DAG containing N nodes
and E edges, the computational complexity of each GCN
layer typically amounts to O(N + E), while the multihead
attention mechanism in the Transformer requires pairwise
similarity computation among all nodes in the worst case,
yielding O(N2) complexity. Given L GCN layers, the total
complexity of this phase approximates O(L(N + E)) +
O(N2). Te node selection phase employs a three-layer FC
neural network to score and normalize each candidate node.
With constant-scale network parameters, the processing
complexity for N nodes remains O(N), rendering this phase
computationally lightweight. Te processor allocation phase
adopts a hybrid heuristic algorithm within a rolling window
framework to reduce decision space, selecting optimal
processors for unscheduled nodes. For a system with P
processors, a window size w(w ≤P), and an average pre-
decessor count d per node, evaluating each processor re-
quires

d

O(w · d)

for N nodes becomes O(N · w · d).
In practical scenarios, where w and d are typically small
constants, this phase approximates O(N). Te total online
scheduling
complexity
thus
combines
as

Crucially, the quadratic complexity scaling O(N2) inherent
to Transformer architectures constitutes the asymptotic bot-

structural decomposition into vertex selection and resource
binding phases, the decoupling strategy achieves polynomial
reduction in action space cardinality while optimizing com-
putational load distribution across heterogeneous processors.
Te training phase (employing PPO and A3C-based re-
inforcement learning) involves iterative policy updates and
gradient computations, with its complexity primarily confned
to ofine optimization and remaining independent of real-time
scheduling operations.

Below are their methodological summaries:
1. FCFS-QL-HEFT: A hybrid task scheduling method

the classical heuristic HEFT algorithm. It employs Q-
learning for task sequencing while retaining HEFT’s

2. FCFS-HDPSO: A metaheuristic approach combining

mitigate premature convergence to local optima.
3. Smart-mDAG-EFT: A DRL-based intelligent sched-
uling framework for multi-DAG workloads, aiming to
minimize makespan. Its architecture comprises two
components: a dependency-aware feature extraction
module and a cascaded neural network structure for
decision-making.
4. EDA-GA: A hybrid evolutionary algorithm merging
estimation of distribution algorithms (EDA) with
genetic algorithms (GA). EDA generates candidate
solutions via probabilistic modeling and sampling,
while GA expands search diversity through crossover
and mutation operations, collectively optimizing
scheduling policies.
5. DRLBTSA: A DRL-based task scheduling algorithm
utilizing a deep Q-network (DQN) to dynamically
adjust scheduling strategies by integrating task pri-
orities and real-time resource states.
Crucially, QL-HEFT and HDPSO methodologies were
inherently architected for unitary workfow scheduling
contexts. To facilitate comparative analysis in multi-DAG
scheduling
environments,

a FCFS queuing discipline.

5.4. Comparative Analysis of Experimental Results
5.4.1. Analysis of Hyperparameters in Experimental Simu-
lation Models. Hyperparameters have a crucial impact on
the training optimization of the A2C algorithm in the
scheduling process. Tese hyperparameters include the re-
ward weight λ, learning rate α, discount rate c, and clipping
factor ε.
Te reward weight λ refects the average loss of the
scheduling algorithm under diferent real-world constraint
conditions; hence, it also afects the scheduling algorithm.
Te average loss in the reward weight λ consists of system
average loss in terms of time consumption and power
consumption, calculated according to equation (19). Al-
though the present study employs three empirical values to
balance latency and energy consumption, this selection is by
no means arbitrary; rather, it is grounded in a system
sensitivity analysis (as demonstrated by the experiments
below) and corroborated by existing literature. Te approach
is consistent, in theory, with common practices in multi-
objective reinforcement learning. As reported by Farsang
et al. and Zhang et al. [34], when latency and energy bear
comparable—but
not
overwhelmingly
domi-
nant—importance, practitioners typically favor intermediate
weightings. Tese studies likewise emphasize that extreme λ
values tend to bias the policy toward single-objective op-
timization, which may degrade overall system performance.
Accordingly, in the present experiments, we selected three
representative λ values for simulation and analysis.
Te learning rate α and the discount rate c are important
hyperparameters in reinforcement learning, with suitable α
and c determining the convergence of the objective function
to a local optimum. ε is an important parameter in the PPO
algorithm, used to control the step size during policy up-
dates, ensuring that the policy does not undergo signifcant
changes during each update.
Firstly, hyperparameter experimental analysis follows
the principle of comparative experiments: only one exper-
imental variable is altered per experiment, while other
factors remain at optimal conditions. From Figure 5(a), it
can be seen that when λ is 0.6, the performance is optimal,
and the loss is minimal. Terefore, 0.6 is chosen as the value
for the hyperparameter λ. From Figure 5(b), it is evident that
when α  0.0001, the training is the most stable and the
reward value is the highest. Training with α  0.00001 starts
well but becomes unstable, while training with α  0.001 is
too slow and unsuitable. Similarly, from Figure 5(c), it can be
seen that when c  0.95, the training performs well, and the
results for the other two parameter values resemble the
learning rate’s training result curves. Figure 5(d) shows that
the clipping factor formed by a nonlinear logarithmic decay
function yields better results than a linear one, with an initial
clipping factor value of 0.1 performing better than an initial
value of 0.5. Terefore, the comparative experiments in
Section 5.4.2 are conducted under the conditions of λ  0.6,
α  0.001, c  0.95, and ε_log  0.1.

6. Conclusion
Tis research introduces a deep reinforcement scheduling
framework targeting heterogeneous many-core architec-
tures, engineered to concurrently orchestrate polymorphic
workloads while minimizing schedule length. Te meth-
odology implements a tri-phase optimization pipeline: to-
pological feature abstraction, vertex discernment, and
resource binding.
Within the feature abstraction phase, an AdapterFusion-
augmented transformer module synergizes with graph
convolutional operators to distill critical path semantics and
internode dependencies, signifcantly enhancing represen-
tational efcacy. Te vertex discernment stage employs
feedforward networks to compute task priority logits,
normalized via Softmax activation for stochastic sampling.
Processor
allocation
leverages
temporal-window
con-
strained heuristics incorporating task replication strategies
to optimize resource mapping.
Policy optimization utilizes PPO with adaptive clipping
decay, embedded within an advantage A2C paradigm. Tis
dual-mechanism maintains exploration entropy while ac-
celerating
gradient
convergence.
Empirical
validation
demonstrates
statistically
signifcant
improvements
in
schedule length reduction, concurrency utilization, and
solution stability, establishing Pareto dominance in multi-
objective scheduling space.
Te proposed algorithm demonstrates broad applica-
bility across diverse scenarios and accommodates DAGs of
arbitrary topological structures. While benchmark datasets
exhibit comprehensive coverage, they lack domain-specifc
partitioning.
Te
scheduling
process
employs
batch-
processing paradigms for data transmission, omitting
consideration of stream-processing mechanisms. System
reliability remains underexplored: escalating processor
counts and task volumes exacerbate interprocessor resource
contention, yet no fault tolerance mechanisms or failure
recovery architectures are designed to address these dy-
namics. Additionally, the implementation disregards critical
hardware-specifc factors such as communication frequency,
processor load fuctuations, idle power consumption, and
DVFS, which collectively induce more intricate energy
consumption patterns in real-world systems. Tese dis-
crepancies from practical operational environments con-
stitute pivotal research directions for future algorithmic
refnement and system co-design.
