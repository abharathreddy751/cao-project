## 3. Problem and motivation

A scientific workflow is a DAG of tasks with file dependencies. Each ready task can run on one of several resources whose speed, contention and transfer costs differ. A scheduler that knows execution times can place tasks better, but predicting costs time and can be wrong. Published learned schedulers are mostly evaluated in simulation, and several do not measure their own decision cost (see section 6). Our own project has real-hardware evidence that better prediction accuracy gave only small scheduling gains, because prediction and scheduling overhead matter. This project turns that observation into a controlled study on a real, recognised workflow.

## 4. Research question

Under what workload and resource conditions does execution-time prediction improve dynamic DAG scheduling on heterogeneous resources after accounting for prediction, scheduling, queueing and data-transfer overhead?

## 5. Contribution and hypotheses

**Primary contribution (one):** a controlled, overhead-accounted, real-hardware study of how much predictor sophistication improves actual DAG scheduling of a real scientific workflow, using decision-time-safe data and a ladder from no prediction to learned models.

**Supporting contributions (at most two):**
1. Generalisation of predictors and schedulers to workflow instances and sizes not seen in training (leave-instance-out evaluation).
2. Transfer-aware placement to a remote or GPU pool, evidenced on this machine only as **[emulation]** (remote) and **[real]** (GPU, separate workload).

**Primary hypothesis (falsifiable, no promised size):** prediction-aware placement beats the best non-predictive baseline only when the runtime ratios between resources and the contention are large relative to prediction cost, scheduling cost and timing noise. Otherwise a simple method matches it.

**Evidence that would support it:** a paired-block makespan advantage whose confidence interval excludes zero in the high-ratio/high-contention conditions and not in the low conditions, after overhead is counted. **Evidence that would reject it:** no condition where predictive placement beats the strongest non-predictive baseline, or a gain that disappears once decision cost is counted. A null result is an accepted, reportable outcome.

**Not claimed:** a new learning algorithm, a new list-scheduling algorithm, or novelty of "task graph + queues + eligibility" (the base patent already claims that, see section 7). The proposed scheduler is a **study instrument**: rank-based priority plus predicted, queue-aware, transfer-aware earliest-finish-time placement.

Directions compared (all against the sources in section 6): (1) prediction-sophistication study, **chosen**; (2) a new joint priority/resource/transfer scheduler, rejected as primary because HEFT, PEFT, CPOP, LACHESIS and StarPU already occupy that space; (3) workload generalisation or online adaptation, kept only as supporting question 1.

## 7. Base patent: what it covers

US 11,561,826 B1, Xilinx, filed 2020-11-12, granted 2023-01-24, 20 claims, listed active with an adjusted expiry of 2041-07-07 (catalogue data, not legal advice). The 20 claims were read VERBATIM on 2026-10-13 (RENDERED-DOM: the claims section of the Google Patents page, parsed in the built-in browser; text and method in [SOURCES_VERBATIM.md](SOURCES_VERBATIM.md) section 1). The PDF was not read. This is a text reading, not legal advice and not a freedom-to-operate opinion.

Claimed: kernel objects tied to compute circuits; a task graph with data-dependency edges; per-node assignment to one or more kernel objects; task queues; threads that enqueue and dequeue by assignment; blocking and non-blocking kernels; several graphs feeding one queue; queue sharing; configurable threads per kernel; performance measurement adjusting thread count; output-buffer addresses; in-degree counters; utilisation-triggered thread increase; a report function; graph creation while running.

Not found in the claims (keyword absence): machine learning, prediction, execution-time estimation, performance model, recommendation, dynamic offloading, named hardware (CPU, GPU, FPGA). **Verbatim check, 2026-10-13 (SOURCES_VERBATIM.md section 1.3):** my own case-insensitive keyword search on the 20 verbatim claim strings found 0 occurrences of machine learning, predict*, estimat*, model, offload*, GPU, CPU and FPGA in claims 1-20 (also 0 for learn* and neural); this replaces the earlier second-hand fetch-tool check. **Caveats ([LITERATURE_UPDATE.md](LITERATURE_UPDATE.md) section 1):** (1) the patent TITLE and ABSTRACT on Google Patents name machine learning tasks ("Scheduling processing of machine learning tasks on heterogeneous compute circuits"): they name machine-learning tasks as the workload being scheduled, not an ML-based prediction method, and the word does not occur in the claims. The description also uses ML-platform language (Caffe and TensorFlow, seen in the fetched description text). (2) Absence of a word does not decide whether a feature is within a claim's scope; no claim construction was done. (3) Source of the text: the markup Google Patents serves for the URL, read in the browser; the Google PDF returned HTTP 403 and the USPTO PDF has no text layer. Still not legal advice; no freedom-to-operate opinion is implied. The "one kernel, one queue" rule and "a thread dequeues only tasks assigned to its kernel" are in the description, not the claims.

What this means for us:
- A task graph, eligibility-gated dispatch, shared queues and utilisation-based scaling are already in the patent, so they cannot be presented as ours.
- Prediction-driven placement is not in the claims. That is an observation, not a freedom-to-operate opinion; we make no legal claim.
- The survey workbook's Project_Summary ("lightweight ML model recommends a resource, dynamic offloading") is **not** in the claims.
- B4 remains a labelled comparison baseline (`docs/B4_PATENT_MAPPING.md`, which was written from the abstract and description; its caveat that claims were not retrieved is narrowed by this paraphrase check).
